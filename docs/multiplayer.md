# Multiplayer

How online games work for the players, and how to run and host the relay they connect through. The game itself is described in [game.md](game.md).

## Playing online

In the client, open **Multiplayer**, connect to the relay address (default `ws://localhost:8080`), then create or join a room.

- **Rooms:** choose Classic or Event Horizon, free-for-all or two-team play (everybody picks their own team in the room, the host can deal everybody out again with *Reset teams*) and the rules of the game (rounds, planets, reflective edges, shot power, flight time, betting on the round, …). Both modes support up to six players. Rooms can be protected with a password.
- **Host:** the host can change the rules while the room is waiting and starts the game. The host runs the authoritative simulation and relays state patches to the other players – only what changed since the last tick, so a match needs a few KB/s instead of MB/s. Guests carry a shot's flight on between patches, so it moves smoothly.
- **Event Horizon online:** everybody aims at the same time against one shared clock.
- **Oracle:** whoever is shot down does not just wait. Turn by turn the oracle asks "Will Lena hit?", with simultaneous shots (Event Horizon, or Classic with *Simultaneous shots*) "Who gets hit?". One card at the bottom edge asks the question and takes the tip until the shots are away; right tips pay 100 / 150 (a named ship) / 75 (nobody), and every right tip in a row raises the multiplier up to ×3. The oracle's own standing is a side standing – it never counts towards winning – and the oracle is always on in online games. A wrong tip costs nothing but the streak; the one exception is a bet on the round, below, which the host has to allow. The host judges the tips, so a relay older than this feature rejects them. On top of that:
  - **Face down:** the other shot-down players' tips lie face down on the card ("● Dora ?") – everybody sees *that* someone tipped, nobody sees *what* – and turn over one after the other when the shots are away, so there is nothing to copy. The wire carries them face down as well – only who tipped, plus the hit/miss tally behind the mood below. A right tip against the crowd (with at least three tipsters, fewer than half named the same) pays 50 on top.
  - **The ghosts' mood:** the living see how the ghosts lean on the shot in the air – a ring and a note over the shooter's ship ("The ghosts don't believe in you (3 of 4)"), not who tipped what. When the majority of the tipsters turns out wrong the field announces *Oracle fooled!*; when three or more all agreed and were right, *The oracle knew!*
  - **Who wins the round?:** as soon as somebody is out and two or more ships are still in, a second question opens as a quieter row of the same card and stays open for the rest of the round. Tip with Shift + 1–5 or by tapping, as long as the next shots have not flown (changing your mind is fine); a tip pays 100 for every rival the winner had to beat when the tip was made (two ships left pay 100, five pay 400), a draw tipped as "nobody" pays 200. With teams, the whole winning team wins. The others only see how many have tipped; after the round the answers are turned over on the card.
  - **Betting on the round:** with the room rule *Betting on the round* switched on (the host sets it while the room is waiting; it is off by default, since it moves points of the match), a shot-down player who has points can put some of them on their tip on the round question – once per round, after tipping. The stakes are a share of the score: half of the most allowed, or the most, which is half of the score (at least 20). They leave the score at once and **do count towards the match**. All the stakes of a round make one pot: whoever tipped right shares it in proportion to their stakes, whoever tipped wrong loses theirs. If nobody was right, everybody was, or only one bet, everybody just gets their stake back. Payouts come in steps of ten; the odds are whatever the other bettors make them – the pot is shown beside the stake, and what everybody tipped stays face down until the round is over. At the end of the round the card lists the bets, and what they came to goes into the scores and the history chart.
  - **Honours:** the round's and the match's best get a title – *Prophet* (most points), *Seer* (longest run of right tips), *Lone wolf* (right against the crowd), *Optimist* / *Pessimist* (almost always "hit" / "miss"), *Unlucky* (longest run of wrong tips) – but only when the lead is clear. At the end of a round the card turns into the round's summary – who won the round question, everybody's tip turned over, the standings and the honours, one at a time – and the honours are listed on the final screen.
- **Ghost lane (planet smasher):** a second way to pass the time once you are shot down, and the round is not yet decided. About three seconds after your exit the hint *Ghost lane [G]* appears where "Waiting for the others" stood; **G** opens a semi-transparent panel in the bottom right corner (**E** or the button in its header makes it bigger) with a small field of rocks, a few planets and your ship as a shadow in your colour. Aim and fire as usual, five shots per lane, and a dotted line shows the first stretch of the flight – long in the first lanes, shorter with every one. A shot blows up every rock it touches, and every blast sets off the rocks within its reach a moment later, so a whole cluster goes up in a chain; **bombs** (red) have a much wider blast, a shot that ends on a **planet** blows the planet up – and with it the gravity that bent your shots – and a **golden rock** is worth a lot. The n-th thing one shot blows up pays more than the one before (up to ×4), long chains pay a bonus, and every swing-by raises the shot's total by half. The lane is cleared when every **glowing core** is gone: that pays a bonus plus a reward for every shot left (and for blowing up everything), and brings up the next lane at once. Every lane cleared in a row raises the multiplier by 0.5 up to ×3; a lane lost ends the series. Every fourth lane has a black hole, which swallows shots and feeds on rocks that go up near it. Every lane has a known way through each cluster, so none is out of reach. Everyone flies the same lanes: the seed comes from the round number and the world the round was dealt, which every screen has, so nothing extra travels over the network. The panel closes when the next round starts and rests during the killcam and between rounds, so Enter and Space still skip it. Nothing counts towards the match; it can be switched off in the settings, per device.
- **Lobby:** it remembers your server, name and room settings (separate from the settings of offline games) and reconnects on its own. Leaving a game puts you back into the lobby.

## Running the relay

Start the relay with `npm run dev:server` (needs the Rust toolchain; `PORT` overrides `8080`).

Set `ALLOWED_ORIGINS` to the frontend's exact origin before startup. For Vite, run `ALLOWED_ORIGINS=http://localhost:5173 npm run dev:server`; `npm run dev:server` defaults to localhost origins on ports 5173 and 4173 for development. Other deployments must configure an allowlist; the relay refuses to start without one unless `ALLOW_ALL_ORIGINS=1`.
The Rust relay logs through `tracing`. Set `RUST_LOG=debug` to show each message and room event. The default level (`info`) shows lifecycle events.

**Hosting.** For remote players, host both the web client and the relay at reachable addresses. Use a `wss://` address behind TLS when the client is served over HTTPS.

**Relay environment.** `PORT` selects the listening port (default `8080`). `ALLOWED_ORIGINS` is a comma-separated exact-origin allowlist; `ALLOW_ALL_ORIGINS=1` disables origin checks. Set both `TLS_CERT` and `TLS_KEY` to PEM file paths to serve WSS directly; if TLS terminates at a reverse proxy, leave both unset and forward to the relay over a trusted network. `MAX_CONNECTIONS` (8192), `MAX_CONNECTIONS_PER_IP` (64), `CONNECTIONS_PER_IP_PER_SECOND` (20), and `MAX_MESSAGES_PER_SECOND` (300) tune admission and per-connection rate limits. These limits use the TCP peer address; configure proxy/network limits accordingly. `RUST_LOG` controls log filtering. `npm run bench:relay -- ws://localhost:8080` sends the localhost Vite origin by default; override it with `BENCH_ORIGIN` when benchmarking another allowlisted origin.
